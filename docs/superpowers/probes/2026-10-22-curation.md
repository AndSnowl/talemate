### Where it fails

**1. False moves are relation claims about the entry itself.** 30 of the 31 create false moves name the entry being written:
- "The Church of the Eternal Light maintains that the Divine Right of the Tsar is the cornerstone of Moscavian theology…" was filed on the Church;
- "Deepcarving is classified as an advanced Technique within the broader field of Runeweaving…" was filed on Runeweaving, in 8 of 8 Deepcarving runs;
- "Unlike Firescribing … Deepcarving produces permanent inscriptions…" was filed on Firescribing.

The placement probe saw the same thing ("many are relations"). On accept, each would put a fact about the doctrine onto the Church, which is the scattering the spec calls an anti-goal.

A code filter on the entry's own name doesn't separate these from right items: 37 of the 44 right create items name the entry too.

The repeat detector (0.4, against the target and its reach-1 neighbours) catches 11 of the 31. That leaves 20 false of 56 ticked items (36%). These numbers come from `scripts/dev/probe_curation_repeats.py`, results in the raw JSON's `repeats`.

**The loose reading.** Almost all of these are relation claims: they tie the entry being written to another entry ("X regards the Illumination as the cornerstone of its theology", "Deepcarving demands more energy … in accordance with the Doctrine of Cost"). If such a claim may live on either entry, only 2 of the 31 stay false:
- Divine Right r2/0: "The Divine Right of the Tsar is the doctrinal cornerstone upon which the Church's relationship with the state is built, asserting that the Tsar's authority is … sacred", filed on the Church. This is the doctrine's own content.
- Illumination r3w/1: "The Illumination is also known in some theological texts as the Blinding Transformation", filed on the event stub. This is a name of the Illumination itself.

One more is borderline. Divine Right r1/1, "…the Tsar serves as the Eternal Light's visible representative on earth", attributes the doctrine's content to the Church; counting it false gives 3/75. The loose count is 2–3/75 (3–4%), or 2–3/313 of all facts.

**The denominator.** Over all facts written (238 own + 75 elsewhere), the strict count is 31/313 (10%), comparable to the placement probe's false moves (A2 3%, A 8%) and over its bar. Under the loose reading it is 2/313.

**2. The Illumination retells the species and the realm: about 45% on topic at every reach.** The doctrine's subject is an event, and the model writes the event's whole consequence:
- pure-line and eared plumage;

Every one of these facts has a home in the list (Moscavian Owl-folk, the Tsardom). The model files a few in elsewhere and keeps the rest as facts. The repeat detector flags 32 of the 44 off-topic Illumination facts, against the Owl-folk, the Tsardom and the races. So in a preview they would arrive unticked.

It also flags 24 of the 38 on-topic Illumination facts, and 22 of the 47 on-topic Divine Right facts, mostly because they restate the stub's drawer line or its mentions. The detector would untick them too (`repeats` in the raw JSON).

**3. The Conclave filed Runeweaving's Deepcarving, the Doctrine of Cost and the Codex as its items.

The core's elsewhere items are almost all right (77/82), but they are copies: 72 of the 77 restate a fact the target already holds (detector at 0.4). ### v3: one more rule (r2, 8 calls)

I added one rule, against the relation claims. These lines are untested at r1 and r3, and untested on the core:
- **create:** `- A claim that ties another entry to the {{ kind }} ("it is taught at X", "unlike X, it...", "X uses it") is the {{ kind }}'s own: it goes in "facts". "elsewhere" is only for what another entry's page should say even if the {{ kind }} didn't exist.`
- **Fill:** `- "elsewhere" is only for what another entry's page should say even if {{ name }}'s new items didn't exist: a claim about one of your new items, or tying another entry to one, is no elsewhere claim.`

**Which expected targets were missing from the list:**

**None was missed for want of a culture/cultures, owner, event-place or species-origin link.** No thing in this world has any of those fields set (`field_values` holds none). The Codex has no owner, the events are stubs with no place, and the species has no origin field. So this world can't answer the open question either way. Every miss above is the cap at work, not a missing link: at r3w (24 entries) the Divine Right list holds all six expected targets. *My call:* add them as links when Task 1 is next touched. They are thing-valued fields like `heritage`, and nothing here argues against them.

**The cap cuts ties by name, and steps don't count.**
- A sibling, reached through `part_of` twice, scores 1.0 × 1.0 = 1.0. A direct reference scores 0.6, so for a stub filed in a sphere, every sibling outranks every entry its own facts name.
- A three-step path such as 1.0 × 1.0 × 0.6 ties a direct reference at 0.6. Among ties, `near()` sorts by name.

So at r3 with 12 entries, the Illumination's list dropped Moscavian Owl-folk, Moskov and the Tsardom (all direct references, 0.6) for Eternal Light, Free Cities, Frosted Wing and Great Synod. Those come first alphabetically at the same 0.6.

The same rule cut the White Talon from the Tsardom Governance Fill's list at r1 and r2: "W" sorts after the twelfth name.

**What each fix recovers** (code only: `scripts/dev/probe_curation_ranking.py`, results in the raw JSON's `ranking`). The count is expected targets missing from the lists, summed over the 9 cases and 4 variants, with the White Talon (Tsardom Fill) and Moskov (Illumination) watched too.

| Scheme | Missing | Recovers |
|---|---|---|
| as run | 24 | – |
| tie-break: steps, then direct references, then name | 16 | the White Talon at every variant; the Illumination's Owl-folk, Tsardom and Moskov at r3; Snowy Owls at r3w |
| part_of weight 0.7 (a sibling scores 0.49) | 19 | the Illumination at r3 and r3w; the White Talon at r3w only |
| at most 3 siblings or parts | 21 | Eared Owls for the Illumination at r2; Owl-folk and Moskov at r3 |
| 0.7 + tie-break | 16 | as the tie-break |
| 3 siblings + tie-break | 15 | as the tie-break, plus Eared Owls at r2 |

**The tie-break fixes the alphabetical cuts. Neither sibling fix recovers the r2 misses that matter.** The Divine Right's Snowy Owls and Zakonnik, and the Illumination's Snowy Owls, sit at 0.36: two hops of 0.6, with no direct link. About 11 other entries tie with them there. Freeing sibling slots fills them with the alphabetically first 0.36 entries (Eared, Free Cities, Frosted Wing, Keshra), and with no direct references the tie-break can't order them. They come in only at 24 entries, or with a rule this probe didn't test, such as counting two-hop paths.

*My call for Task 5 (or a small Task 1 follow-up):* break score ties by fewer steps, then by direct references, then by name. Add a cap of 3 siblings (it costs nothing and helps a little). A lower sibling weight adds nothing on top of the tie-break here.

### Verdict: FALLBACK

**Strictly**, none of the three prompts meets 80% right with ≤5% false moves on elsewhere items. **Loosely**, create (3%) and Fill (0%) meet the false bar. Create's own facts reach 74% (88% without the Illumination) and the core's 60% (78% without the conflated Eternal Light).

The spec's fallback is "fall back to the whole-world list for small worlds, and stop to rethink with the owner". The whole-world list isn't the fix, though. The failure is the same with a whole-world list: the placement probe's B items were relations too. What the owner should weigh:
- **Create:**
  - v1 is 74% of own facts on topic, 88% without the Illumination; v3 is 78%.
  - Strictly, a quarter (v3) to two fifths (v1) of elsewhere items are false.
  - Loosely, 0–4% are, since nearly all of them are relation claims.
- **Fill:** 91% on topic. The post-hoc filter takes its strict false moves to 3%, a pass to be confirmed on a fresh run. Loosely, none are false.
- **The sphere core:** elsewhere adds nothing new (94% copies) and costs reply tokens up to the cap. *My call:* don't add elsewhere to the core. Keep it for create and Fill.

### Wording, if the owner goes ahead (create-thing and Fill, as tested)

Variables:
- `nearby`: a list of candidate lines, built as above.
- `part_of_now`: `"<sphere>, in its <drawer> drawer"`, or `""`.
- `part_of_sphere`: the sphere's name.

**create-thing.jinja2.** Block 1, just before `The world's calendar:` (after the spheres block):

```
{%- if nearby %}

Entries near this one in the world:
{% for line in nearby -%}
- {{ line }}
{% endfor %}
{%- endif %}
```

Block 2, the example object (the generic branch's facts and names):

```
"text": "One claim, in a full sentence.", "since": "YEAR"{% if nearby %}, "held_by": null{% endif %}}, {"category": "{{ categories[-1] }}", "text": "Another claim."{% if nearby %}, "held_by": null{% endif %}}], "names": [{"name": "...", "kind": "place", "aka": []}]{% if nearby %}, "elsewhere": [{"entry": "<an entry's name>", "text": "A claim mainly about that entry, in a full sentence.", "held_by": null}]{% endif %}
```

Block 3, the rules, before `{%- if spheres %}` / `- "ties"`. The `held_by` line is only if probe 4's fallback is overruled.

```
{%- if nearby %}
- Keep "facts" to claims mainly about the {{ kind }} itself: a claim mainly about another entry goes in "elsewhere".
- "elsewhere": after the names, what you would add about other entries, each {"entry", "text", "held_by"}: "entry" the entry's name as the list of entries near this one writes it, or a new name you also list in "names"; "text" one claim in a full sentence. Never the {{ kind }} itself. An empty list is fine.
- A claim that ties another entry to the {{ kind }} ("it is taught at X", "unlike X, it...", "X uses it") is the {{ kind }}'s own: it goes in "facts". "elsewhere" is only for what another entry's page should say even if the {{ kind }} didn't exist.
{%- endif %}
```

**sphere-fill.jinja2.**

Block 1, the same `nearby` block, before `{%- if existing %}`.

Block 2, the example's end becomes:

```
"names": [{"name": "...", "kind": "place", "aka": []}]{% if nearby %}, "elsewhere": [{"entry": "<an entry's name>", "text": "A claim mainly about that entry, in a full sentence.", "held_by": null}]{% endif %}}
```

Block 3, the rules, before `No commentary`:

```
{%- if nearby %}
- Keep "new_items" to what belongs in {{ drawer_title }}: a claim mainly about another entry goes in "elsewhere".
- "elsewhere": after the names, what you would add about other entries, each {"entry", "text", "held_by"}: "entry" the entry's name as the list of entries near this one writes it, or a new name you also list in "names"; "text" one claim in a full sentence. Never {{ name }} itself. An empty list is fine.
- "elsewhere" is only for what another entry's page should say even if {{ name }}'s new items didn't exist: a claim about one of your new items, or tying another entry to one, is no elsewhere claim.
```
