# Probe: sphere core, Fill, ties and context in play

Date: 2026-10-22. Model: koboldcpp, Skyfall-31B-v4.2-i1-Q4_K_M, through Talemate's own KoboldCpp client (the `--ai` app's config, default agent settings), each sphere prompt sent through `Prompt.request` inside `world_ai_call`. Calls ran one at a time: cores took 25–48 s, Fill calls 8–13 s, character creates 25–35 s and narrator turns 10–16 s. This is Spheres Task 3. It settles the reply shapes and prompt wording for the core, Fill, ties and the scene's key-facts block, which Tasks 4, 6 and 7 copy.

- Script: `scripts/dev/probe_spheres.py` (`--only 1 2 2g 2n 3 4`, `--dry`).
- Raw output: `raw/2026-10-22-spheres.json`. It holds 46 recorded calls (prompt and reply), each reply's parse and KoboldCpp token count, the ties labels, the three World blocks and a keyword scan of each narrator turn.
- `core_nested` holds the first two core replies, in the nested shape the brief suggested. Both were malformed (see Probe 1), so the run was stopped and the core changed to a flat shape. One more nested call finished on the server after the stop and wasn't recorded, so the total is 47 calls.
- Probe 2's `2g` and `2n` are follow-ups, added after Fill repeated existing items.

## Setup

Two worlds, present year 1020, built by the script:
- **fresh:**
  - the Owl-folk species, whose origin fact names the Tsardom (994, the Night of Feathers), its Snowy race, and Humans;
  - the **Tsardom of Moscavia** (a place, with the everyday name "Moscavia"), with five facts. One of them is "[The White Dawn] is the Tsardom's state church, and teaches that the Night of Feathers was a purification";
  - **Kremova**, its capital;
  - **the White Dawn**, a `sphere` stub with template `religion`.
- **full:** the same, but the White Dawn is a whole religion sphere:
  - its drawers are Doctrines, Hierarchy, Rites and Ceremonies, People, Holy Sites and Timeline;
  - its `about` fact says it is the state church and that the Night of Feathers was a purification;
  - **Doctrines** holds two statements (snowy owl-folk are the purest; the Night of Feathers was a purification) and the Doctrine of Separation (lore: the faithful may not marry or share a roost with humans);
  - **Hierarchy** holds the Council of Elders (lore: seven snowy elders in Kremova, who meet at every new moon) and the statement "Dawn-priests lead each parish…";
  - **Rites** holds the Dawn Vigil (lore: silent watching of first light on high galleries) and the feather-offering statement;
  - **Holy Sites** holds the Pale Spire, a place in Kremova;
  - its timeline is 870, 994, 1007 and 1019 (the Doctrine of Separation).

  The full world also has **Moscavian culture**, a `culture` sphere with four customs and beliefs statements, the Feather Dance (lore) and a 1015 timeline fact, "The Feather Dance was banned in Kremova by the White Dawn".

The context blocks come from the real Connections helpers:
- `gather_mentions` and `_mention_lead` give "Mentioned elsewhere" and "About those";
- `_found_in_prompt` and `_named_in_prompt` give "Named in this prompt";
- `suggested_prompt` gives the stub's prompt.

Spheres and drawers come from `world/spheres.py`: `items`, `drawers`, `timeline` and `key_facts`.

## Probe 1: the core

The core is one sphere per template, 2 runs each, under `creative_instruction_2048`:
- **religion:** the White Dawn, expanded from its stub with `suggested_prompt`;
- **nation:** the Tsardom, promoted, with its facts given as "Already known about it";
- **order:** "The Imperial Academy of Runecraft in Kremova…";
- **culture:** "Moscavian culture…";
- **field:** "Runic magic…";
- **other:** "The Amber Road: the river trade that carries Moscavian amber south…".

The prompt gives the template's `TEMPLATE_DRAWERS` (less people, events and branches, which the app keeps) as starting drawers, plus the five writable types with a one-line gloss each.

**The nested shape broke** (`core_nested`, 2 of 2 replies). In it, each drawer carries its own `"items": [...]`. Both replies ran to the end (1808 and 1217 tokens, not truncated), but in both the model forgot the `]` that closes `"drawers"` before `"names"`. So the outer object doesn't parse: `json_objects` recovers each drawer, but the name, summary, aka and timeline are lost. Texts also ran long: a timeline entry ran to 40 words or more.

**The flat shape worked: 12 of 12 replies complete.** In it, `"drawers": [{"type", "title"}]` is followed by `"items": [{"drawer": "<title>", …}]`, with "one short sentence, under 20 words".

| | |
|---|---|
| Reply tokens | 876–1658 (mean 1352) |
| Drawers per sphere | 4–6, all of valid types; every item named an existing drawer title |
| Items per drawer | 2–5 (one drawer had 5), mostly 4: the model takes the top of "2 to 4" |
| Named items vs statements | 179 items: 158 named (lore 100, place 33, event 19, `practice` 4, item 2), only 21 statements |
| Timeline | 4 entries each, in order, no year after 1020 |

**Drawer types and titles were sensible and specific:**
- **religion:** Doctrines, Rites and Ceremonies, Holy Sites, Hierarchy;
- **nation:** Lands, Customs, "Laws & Doctrine", Government, and once Learning;
- **order:** "Ranks and Offices", "Fields of Study" (knowledge), "Rites and Customs", "Sites and Territory";
- **field:** two `knowledge` drawers, Principles and Frontier, as the template has them, plus Techniques;
- **culture:** "Rites, Ceremonies and Daily Customs" and "Doctrines, Values and Legends".

**Weak points:**
- **Drawers were added freely.** Both culture runs added a places and a structure drawer (with "The Tsar" and "Roost Wardens" as items). The field runs added beliefs, structure and places. "Add a drawer of another type only if it clearly needs one" didn't hold back the non-agent templates.
- **Too many stubs.** About 13 named items per sphere (53 drawers, 179 items), plus 7–17 `names`, would make 20 or more stubs from one core.
- **Items that already exist:**
  - the nation listed Kremova, The Vesna and "The White Dawn" (the existing sphere) as items;
  - the culture listed "The Tsar" and "The High Priests of the White Dawn".

  Code must match item names against the world (link, don't stub) the way `names` already is.
- **Timeline "now" entries.** 6 of 12 runs end with a 1020 entry that isn't an event: "The current Tsar reigns from Kremova…", "Enrollment has swollen to over 400 students".
- **`names` mistakes:**
  - templates outside the set: `council` 3, `guild` 2, and `office` in the nested runs;
  - kinds outside the set: `order` and `office`, in Fill;
  - "Moscavian" listed as a `race`;
  - month names ("May", "November") listed as events, in Fill;
  - an invented "The Dawn God" sphere in the nation's names.
- **Real-world drift.** The Amber Road's prompt says "Moscavian amber", and nothing matched the demonym to the Tsardom, so no named context was given. Both runs wrote real Russian history: Kiev, the Volga, the Dnieper, Varangians, Vladimir I, Khazaria, Veliky Novgorod. Every other core had world context (the stub's mentions, the promoted facts, or Kremova, Owl-folk or Moscavia matched in its prompt) and stayed in the world. This is a Connections matching gap ("Moscavian" → Moscavia), not a sphere-prompt one.
- **The agent flag never came from the model.** The template set it, as the spec intends.

**`core-after-council`:** the fresh world's White Dawn stub expanded after the Council of Elders was made `part_of` it, with the council and its facts in an "Already part of it" block. 2 runs, 1037–1390 tokens, both complete.

Adoption must match these by name (with or without "The") and treat them as the adopted entry, not a new stub. Both put it in the drawer of the right type (structure), which is useful as the adopted entry's drawer.

## Probe 3: ties and exceptions at character creation

Two Snowy owl-folk characters went through the real `create.preview_thing` (heritage branch), 2 runs each. The `create-thing.jinja2` copy adds:
- the spheres block, before the calendar line: each sphere's label and `about`, then its non-place items labelled S1…S12 across both spheres, each with its drawer title;
- `"ties"` in the example, and two rule lines.

`_named_in_prompt` left spheres out, so the White Dawn showed once, labelled.

- **"doubter"** is Vasha, a courier raised in the White Dawn who keeps the vigils but thinks the snowy-purity talk is nonsense.
- **"bell-ringer"** is "an old snowy owl-folk bell-ringer at the Pale Spire in Kremova, who has danced the Feather Dance every winter of his life".

| Run | Ties | Exceptions (→ label → item) |
|---|---|---|
| doubter 0 | White Dawn, member, "1004"; Moscavian culture, practitioner | "does not believe snowy owl-folk are purer… Doctrine of Separation is a way for elders to hold power" → S4 → the purity statement ✓ |
| doubter 1 | White Dawn, former practitioner, "1994" (!); Moscavian culture, courier, "1996" | lapsed Dawn Vigil → S3 ✓; "rejects the Doctrine of Purity as political fiction" → S4 ✓ |
| bell-ringer 0 | White Dawn, practitioner, 1002; Moscavian culture, member, 994 | none (fits the prompt) |
| bell-ringer 1 | White Dawn, dawn-priest, 1015; Moscavian culture, practitioner, 994 | "lets humans watch the Dawn Vigil from the lower galleries, contrary to the strict separation doctrine" → S1 → Doctrine of Separation ✓ |

**What worked:**
- **Ties:** 8 of 8 went to existing spheres, by the exact name in the block. None was invented, and none was missed (the bell-ringer's culture tie came from the Feather Dance).
- **Exceptions:** all 4 link to the right item. They read well as "but…" lines. The doubter's purity doubt was linked to the purity statement both times, not to the Separation doctrine.

**Problems:**
- **`since`:**
  - it came as a string 4 times ("1004", "1010", "1994", "1996": every tie of the doubter runs) and as a number 4 times (1002, 994, 1015, 994: every tie of the bell-ringer runs);
  - two years are impossible in a 1020 world: 1994 and 1996, both in doubter run 1;
  - culture ties got a birth year or an arbitrary one.

  Parse it like a fact's `since`: a bare number no later than now, else drop it.
- **Exceptions restated in the facts:** 2 of 4 runs did it. Doubter 0 wrote a `ties`-category fact ("attends the Dawn Vigil out of habit rather than conviction…"). Doubter 1 wrote a personality fact ("thinks the talk of snowy owl-folk being purer is nonsense…").
- **No sphere creed was copied** into the facts.
- **Missed exception:** the bell-ringer's Feather Dance (banned by the White Dawn in 1015, a culture timeline fact) never became one, because timelines weren't in the block. That's acceptable: the block shows what a sphere holds, not its history.
- **Real-world drift:** Vologda, Kostroma and Murom appeared as courier towns. This is the same demonym gap as the Amber Road.

## Recommendations

1. **(a) The core** uses the flat shape below, under `creative_instruction_2048`. Ask for "2 or 3 items" (it fills to the top of the range), and the reply then sits around 1000–1400 tokens. Code-side:
   - parse with `json_objects`, picking the object holding `"drawers"`;
   - an item whose `drawer` matches no drawer title goes to Unsorted;
   - unknown item kinds (`rite`, `ceremony`, `custom`, `practice`, `sphere`, `""`) become lore;
   - match item names to existing things (link) and to already-part-of entries (adopt), with or without a leading "The";
   - name templates `council`/`guild` become `order`, and other templates are dropped;
   - a name of kind `order` becomes a `sphere` with template `order`, and `office` becomes lore;
   - drop month names from `names`.
2. **(b) Fill** uses the `new_items` shape below, with statements as `{"text"}`, under `creative_instruction_1024`. Code still drops an item whose normalised name or text equals one already in the sphere.
3. **(c) Ties:** the spheres block plus the `ties` key and rules below. Code-side:
   - resolve `sphere` by name or other name, and drop unknown ones;
   - parse `since` like a fact's (string or number, no later than now, else none);
   - map `rejects` labels to items, and drop an exception whose label doesn't resolve.

   Add the one untested rule line marked below against restating exceptions in the facts. The fallback, if it doesn't hold, is that the owner sees both in the preview and drops one.
4. **(d) Scene context:** the guarded block below. Task 7 should date timeline lines ("In 1019: …"); `spheres.key_facts` returns them undated today. It should also drop the "Name: " prefix when the item's text already starts with its name, since `_item_line` gives "The Doctrine of Separation: The Doctrine of Separation forbids…".
5. **For Connections, outside this slice:** a demonym ("Moscavian") should match its place ("Moscavia"), or prompts that use it get no world context and drift into real-world history.

## Recommended prompt text

The text below is what ran, except for lines marked *(untested)*. Jinja variables are those of the probe templates (`scripts/dev/probe_spheres.py`: `CORE_TEMPLATE`, `FILL2_TEMPLATE`, `TIES_RULES`, `SPHERES_HEAD`, `GUARDED_LEAD`).

### (a) The core, with its drawer outline

`world/sphere-core.jinja2`, kind `creative_instruction_2048` (**max tokens 2048**; observed 876–1658 at "2 to 4" items):

```
<|SECTION:TASK|>
You create a new {{ label }} for a fantasy world from the owner's short prompt. Treat the prompt as intent, not a spec: invent plausible detail generously to fill it out, turning a rough idea into a fleshed-out one. Keep everything the owner wrote.

Keep everything consistent with itself, with the owner's prompt and with what the world already holds below.

{% if agent -%}
The {{ template }} acts in the world: it decides, rules, declares or teaches, so its timeline is what it has said and done.
{%- else -%}
The {{ template }} is a body of knowledge or custom that is acted upon, so its timeline is how it has changed: what began, changed or was banned, proven or disproven.
{%- endif %}

Owner's prompt:
{{ prompt }}
{%- if known %}

Already known about it (keep these true; they stay on its page):
{% for text in known -%}
- {{ text }}
{% endfor %}
{%- endif %}
{%- if part_of %}

Already part of it (these exist and keep their place in it: write the {{ template }} around them, never list them again as items, and never invent a rival or a second one beside them):
{% for heading, facts in part_of -%}
{{ heading }}:
{% for text in facts -%}
- {{ text }}
{% endfor %}
{%- endfor %}
{%- endif %}
[the Connections blocks exactly as create-thing.jinja2 has them: "Mentioned elsewhere in the world (keep these true):", "About those (…; let the {{ label }} reflect them):", "Named in this prompt (…):"]

Its drawers. A {{ template }} starts with these (type: title):
{% for type, title in start_drawers -%}
- {{ type }}: {{ title }}
{% else -%}
- (none: pick what it needs)
{% endfor %}
The drawer types:
- places: its territory and sites: where it holds sway, is seated, or holy sites
- practices: rites, ceremonies, customs, holidays, techniques
- beliefs: doctrines, values, attitudes, laws, myths and legends
- knowledge: what is known or studied, and its frontier
- structure: ranks, offices, councils, divisions

The world's calendar: it is now {{ now_label }}, year {{ now_year }}{% if era %} of the {{ era }}{% endif %}. The months are {{ month_names | join(", ") }}.
<|CLOSE_SECTION|>
<|SECTION:FORMAT|>
Reply with only one JSON object, shaped exactly like this example:
{"name": "...", "summary": "Two sentences.", "aka": [{"name": "...", "tag": "common", "everyday": true}], "timeline": [{"year": YEAR, "text": "What happened, in one short sentence."}], "drawers": [{"type": "beliefs", "title": "..."}, {"type": "practices", "title": "..."}], "items": [{"drawer": "the drawer's title", "name": "...", "text": "One short sentence.", "kind": "lore"}, {"drawer": "the drawer's title", "text": "A plain statement, in one short sentence."}], "names": [{"name": "...", "kind": "place", "aka": []}]}

- "name": the {{ template }}'s name.{% if has_name %} Use the name already decided: "{{ name }}".{% else %} Invent one if the prompt doesn't give it.{% endif %}
- "summary": two sentences: what it is, whose it is, and what it stands for.
- "timeline": 2 to 4 moments in its history, oldest first, each {"year", "text"}: something that happened in that year, never how things stand now. "year" is a year of this calendar, as a bare number no later than {{ now_year }}; "YEAR" in the example stands for that number.
- "drawers": its drawer outline. Start from the drawers above: keep each that fits, give it a title that suits this {{ template }}, and drop any it doesn't need; add a drawer of another type only if it clearly needs one. "type" is one of: places, practices, beliefs, knowledge, structure. Don't write people, a timeline or branches as drawers: the app keeps those.
- "items": 2 or 3 items for each drawer, drawer by drawer, each naming its drawer by its title in "drawer".
- Each item is {"drawer", "name", "text", "kind"}. When the item is a thing of its own that could get its own entry (a rite, an office, a council, a doctrine, a text, a site), "name" is its proper name, such as "the Dawn Vigil", and "kind" is lore, place (a site), item (an object) or event. For a plain statement ("Priests may not marry."), give only "drawer" and "text". "text" is one short sentence, under 20 words.
- "aka": other names for this {{ template }} itself, if it has any, each {"name", "tag", "everyday"}. "tag" is "proper" for another formal name, or "common" for what people in the world actually call it day to day. Set "everyday" true on the one common name people use most, false on the rest. An empty list is fine.
- "names": after the rest, every proper name your summary, timeline and items use, other than this {{ template }}'s own and its items' own names: people, places, peoples and species, named events, named groups and named objects, new or already in the world. Each is {"name", "kind", "aka"}: "name" in full, as you wrote it; "kind" one of character, place, species, race, sphere, event, item, lore; "aka" any other names you used for the same thing. A faith or church, an order, academy, guild or council, a culture, or a field of knowledge or magic is "sphere", with a "template": religion, order, culture or field, only these (a country or realm is a place; a rank or office is lore). Proper names only: never a description such as "the postmaster" or "a war", nor a title on its own such as "the Tsar", nor a people's adjective such as "Moscavian" on its own. An empty list is fine.

Keep every text to one sentence. No commentary, no markdown fences, just the JSON object.
<|CLOSE_SECTION|>
{{ set_prepared_response('{') }}
```

`label` is `religion (a faith or church)`, `nation`, `order (an academy, guild, order, council or other body)`, `culture`, `field of knowledge or magic`, or `sphere` (for `other`, which also uses `template` = `sphere`). `start_drawers` is `TEMPLATE_DRAWERS[template]` less the people, events and branches types. `agent` is `TEMPLATE_AGENT[template]`.

The *(untested)* changes from the run are:
- "2 or 3 items" (the run said "2 to 4 items");
- the timeline clause "something that happened in that year, never how things stand now";
- the statement form `{"drawer", "text"}` (tested only in Fill's `{"text"}` form; the run's core used `"name": ""`, which never broke in 12 cores but broke in 2 of the 6 Fill replies that used it).
